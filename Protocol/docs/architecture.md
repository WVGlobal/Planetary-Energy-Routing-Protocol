# PERP Architecture

## Architectural Overview

PERP consists of five core layers.

Each layer performs a specific role while remaining independent and interoperable.

---

## Layer 1: Energy Assets

Physical energy resources connect to the network.

Examples include:

- Solar farms
- Wind farms
- Hydroelectric facilities
- Battery storage systems
- Electric vehicles
- Industrial facilities
- Residential systems
- Wireless energy transmitters

Each asset reports operational data to a local node.

---

## Layer 2: Local Energy Nodes

Local nodes aggregate and validate information from connected assets.

Responsibilities include:

- Data collection
- Demand forecasting
- Asset monitoring
- Local optimization
- Security validation

Examples:

- Whanganui Node
- Wellington Node
- Auckland Node
- Sydney Node

---

## Layer 3: Federated AI Mesh

Rather than relying on a single centralized intelligence system, PERP utilizes multiple cooperating AI agents.

Benefits include:

- Fault tolerance
- Scalability
- Reduced centralization risk
- Local decision-making

The mesh exchanges:

- Forecasts
- Risk assessments
- Capacity information
- Routing recommendations

---

## Layer 4: Routing Layer

The routing layer is the decision-making framework responsible for coordinating energy movement across connected infrastructure.

Functions include:

- Supply balancing
- Demand response
- Capacity allocation
- Emergency prioritization
- Route optimization

This layer serves a role analogous to Internet routing protocols.

---

## Layer 5: Governance Layer

The governance layer provides accountability.

Responsibilities include:

- Human oversight
- Auditing
- Compliance
- Conflict resolution
- Ethical safeguards

No automated system may operate without appropriate governance controls.

---

## Guiding Principle

PERP does not seek to centralize control.

PERP seeks to enhance cooperation between autonomous energy systems through open standards and transparent communication.
