# Use Cases

## Renewable Energy Balancing

Solar and wind generation can fluctuate significantly throughout the day.

PERP enables regional nodes to exchange information about energy surpluses and shortages, improving efficiency and reducing waste.

### Example

A solar farm in one region produces excess energy.

PERP identifies available storage capacity elsewhere and coordinates efficient routing and storage decisions.

---

## Community Microgrids

Communities increasingly operate their own local energy systems.

PERP provides a common communication framework allowing microgrids to:

- Share surplus power
- Coordinate storage
- Improve resilience during outages
- Reduce dependence on centralized infrastructure

---

## Disaster Response

Natural disasters can disrupt traditional energy networks.

PERP could prioritize:

- Hospitals
- Emergency services
- Water infrastructure
- Communications networks

during critical events.

---

## Electric Vehicle Networks

Millions of electric vehicles will eventually represent a distributed battery system.

PERP may support coordination between:

- Charging networks
- Vehicle-to-grid systems
- Local energy markets

to improve overall grid stability.

---

## Wireless Energy Infrastructure

Emerging technologies such as long-distance wireless energy transmission could introduce new routing possibilities.

PERP is designed to remain technology-neutral while supporting future energy transport mechanisms.

---

## International Energy Cooperation

Countries may eventually exchange renewable energy resources through interconnected infrastructure.

PERP could provide standard communication protocols that facilitate secure, interoperable coordination across national boundaries.
